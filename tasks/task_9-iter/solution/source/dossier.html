1428 Linden Ridge Road, Cobble Hill

A 1928 brick-and-limestone townhouse by Whitfield & Doré, restored 2019 — neo-Georgian with a Beaux-Arts cornice.

By Marguerite Halloran · Filed Nov 9, 2024 · 14 min read · Brooklyn Bureau
List Price $4,295,000
$ / Sq Ft $1,182
Lot Size 2,178 sf
Bedrooms 5
Bathrooms 3.5
Year Built 1928
HOA None

The first thing you notice on Linden Ridge is the quiet. Two blocks east, Atlantic Avenue is doing its usual Tuesday-afternoon trade, but here, under a corridor of London planes thick enough to file a deed in shade, the city seems to have agreed to keep its voice down. The townhouse at № 1428 has been doing this since 1928 — looking, in the words of its third owner, "like it knows something the rest of the block doesn't."

What it knows, mostly, is restraint. Whitfield & Doré, the partnership behind a string of subdued Brooklyn Heights commissions in the late twenties, designed № 1428 for a textile importer who had wearied of Park Slope's exuberant brownstone. The result is a Flemish-bond façade flattered by limestone quoining, a parlor floor lifted just six steps above the sidewalk, and a service entrance that, ninety-six years on, still latches with a satisfying mechanical thunk.

A renovation that does not announce itself

The 2019 restoration, executed by the Brooklyn architect Esmé Park for the current sellers, is the kind of work that earns its money by being invisible. Park kept the original plaster cornices on the parlor floor, restored the four marble mantels (one of which had been overpainted ten times since the Coolidge administration), and quietly absorbed two new bathrooms into closets that had been doing nothing useful since the seventies.

Mechanicals were rethought entirely. A high-velocity Unico system was threaded through original chase walls, radiant heat was laid under the kitchen and primary bath, and a discreet bank of six solar panels — invisible from the street — provides roughly a third of the house's annual electrical load. None of this announces itself, which is precisely the point.

"It's the rarest sort of trophy listing — one where the trophy is how little it draws attention to itself. You leave thinking you've been in a private library, not a four-and-a-quarter." — Davis Yun, Brown Harris Stevens, who is not the listing broker

How the market read it

№ 1428 came to market on the morning of September 18 at $4,495,000, a number that two of the three brokers we spoke with described as "aspirational but defensible." For ten days it did the things expensive Cobble Hill townhouses are supposed to do — two open houses, three private showings, a write-up in the neighborhood Substack — and then the market did what the market often does when a number is half a percent too brave: it went quiet.

The listing brokerage, Corcoran's Court Street office, cut $200,000 on October 14. Within nine days the house had three competing offers, the highest from a couple relocating from Chicago who, in the words of one of the agents involved, "had stopped looking at anything that wasn't exactly this." A signed contract is expected to be filed with the city by the end of next week.

What the comps say

On a per-square-foot basis, $1,182 places № 1428 squarely in the upper quartile of Cobble Hill trades this year, but below the two limestone outliers on Strong Place that closed in the spring. Lot size is the variable that makes the math interesting: at 2,178 square feet, it is generous for the block, with a south-facing garden deep enough for a mature ginkgo and a small but real kitchen plot.

The HOA line is, for buyers fleeing co-op boards, the headline. There isn't one. The roof is yours, the cornice is yours, the slow argument with the city over the sidewalk vault is yours. Most people we spoke to consider that a feature.

What happens next

The board's pipeline, for now, holds № 1428 in Under Offer. If the contract files on schedule, it will move to In Escrow by the time this dossier goes to print, with closing tentatively set for mid-December. We will update the timeline below as each event prints.
