Tonight's family activity

Build a backyard Jupiter-watching nest 🪐

Cozy up on the lawn, point your binoculars at Jupiter, and try to spot the four little moons Galileo discovered 400 years ago. Takes about 30 minutes.

You'll need

Binoculars (any size) 🧺 A picnic blanket 🛋️ Pillows or cushions Red flashlight Notebook + pencil 🍪 A snack 🧣 Warm clothes

How to do it

  1. 1
    Wait until the sky goes really dark
    About 30 minutes after sunset — around 9:30 PM tonight.
  2. 2
    Spread your blanket facing south
    Use the compass in the sky map above — Jupiter rises high in the southern sky.
  3. 3
    Find the brightest "star" that doesn't twinkle
    Planets shine with a steady light. That's Jupiter!
  4. 4
    Hold your binoculars steady
    Rest your elbows on a fence or your knees to stop the wobble.
  5. 5
    Count the moons
    You should see 2 to 4 little dots near Jupiter. Those are Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto.
  6. 6
    Draw what you see
    Sketch Jupiter and the moons in your notebook. Come back tomorrow — the moons will have moved!
