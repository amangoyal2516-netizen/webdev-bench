About the Civic Dining Program

A municipal initiative of the Office of Food & Culture documenting the city's commons through shared meals, neighborhood pop-ups, and policy that places food at the center of public life.

Mandate & Founding

§ 01 / Establishing Authority
Council Resolution No. 2017-184 · Adopted 12 September 2017 · Effective 01 January 2018
"The Civic Dining Program is hereby established to convene residents around the shared table; to document the foodways of every neighborhood as a matter of public record; and to ensure that the right to eat well, in company, remains a civic concern of this city and not a private one."
— Resolution to Establish a Municipal Civic Dining Program, sponsored by Council District 4 and the Office of Food & Culture, ratified by a vote of 11–2.
Founding Date

01 January 2018

The first municipal program of its kind in the region. Operations commenced with three pilot meals in the West Quarter, Harborline, and Foundry districts during the inaugural quarter.

Convening Authority

Office of Food & Culture

An autonomous office within the Department of Community Affairs, reporting biannually to the Standing Committee on Public Health, Culture & the Commons.

Get Involved

§ 03 / Public Participation

Every meal on the public ledger began with a resident proposal, a venue nomination, or a volunteer signing the standing roster. Three open channels stand at all times.

Channel 01 · Residents

Propose a Meal

Submit a written proposal for a public meal — a neighborhood occasion, a seasonal harvest, a remembrance, or a recipe held by your household. Proposals are reviewed monthly by the program staff.

  • Any resident may submit
  • Reviewed within 30 days
  • No fee, no membership
Open Proposal Form
Channel 02 · Volunteers

Volunteer as a Server

Join the standing volunteer roster and sign up for shifts at upcoming meals. Servers receive a short orientation, food-handling certification, and a civic service letter for hours logged.

  • Shifts of 3 or 4 hours
  • Certification provided
  • Hours count toward civic service
View Volunteer Calendar
Channel 03 · Neighborhoods

Nominate a Venue

Suggest a community center, library hall, courtyard, school cafeteria, place of worship, or any room that holds twenty or more at table. Venues are surveyed for access and added to the public registry.

  • Public or private space
  • Access survey conducted
  • Added to public registry
Open Nomination Form

Notes on Participation

All forms are available in English, Spanish, and Chinese. Paper copies may be requested at the front desk of City Hall, Room 218, during the published office hours of Monday through Thursday, 9:00 to 16:30. Program staff confirm receipt of every written submission within ten working days.
