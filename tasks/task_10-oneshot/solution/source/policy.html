Policy & Ordinances

The Civic Dining Program operates at the intersection of public health code, parks & right-of-way permitting, and procurement rules for community food. This page is a chronological log of every ordinance, resolution, and administrative rule the City Council has adopted in connection with the program — including amendments, repeals, and items still under deliberation.

  1. Ordinance 2025-014 Enacted 18 Sep 2025

    Mobile Hearth Kitchens & Right-of-Way Cooking Permits

    Sponsored by Councilmember Aldona Reyes, District 3 · Co-sponsor: Marcus Okafor, At-Large

    Establishes a tiered permit class for wood-fired and propane-powered mobile cooking installations on city sidewalks and parklets during sanctioned pop-up dinners. Sets clearance, suppression, and acoustic requirements drafted jointly with the Fire Marshal’s Office.

  2. Ordinance 2025-011 Enacted 06 Jun 2025

    Community Meal Procurement & Local Sourcing Floor

    Sponsored by Councilmember Henri Vasquez, District 7

    Requires that no fewer than 55% of ingredients by invoice value at any City-funded community meal be sourced from growers and producers within a 120-mile radius. Provides a hardship waiver process administered by the Food & Culture Office.

    Full text (PDF) Vote record
    In effect
    Vote 11–0
  3. Ordinance 2024-009 Enacted 22 Oct 2024

    Neighborhood Long-Table Authorization (Amendment A)

    Sponsored by Councilmember Priya Thanawala, District 1

    Amends Ordinance 2019-004 to extend long-table street closures from four to six consecutive hours and clarifies noise variance procedures for music programming after 21:00. Adds an explicit accessibility clause requiring step-free seating at every public table.

    Full text (PDF) Vote record
    Amended
    Vote 8–3
  4. Ordinance 2024-007 Enacted 14 May 2024

    Civic Dining Equity & Sliding-Scale Pricing Act

    Sponsored by Councilmember Dawit Berhe, District 5 · Co-sponsor: Aldona Reyes, District 3

    Codifies the three-tier pay-what-you-can pricing model already piloted by the program in 2022–2023, and creates a public reimbursement fund that backfills the difference for participating chefs and venues. Annual appropriation set at $480,000 from the General Fund.

    Full text (PDF) Vote record
    In effect
    Vote 10–1
  5. Ordinance 2023-006 Enacted 03 Sep 2023

    Public Compost & Post-Meal Waste Stream Rules

    Sponsored by Councilmember Lin Hua, District 2

    Mandates separated organic-waste collection at all City-sponsored meals exceeding 60 covers, with hauling contracts awarded through the Department of Public Works. Sets a measurable diversion target of 85% by weight beginning fiscal year 2025.

    Full text (PDF) Vote record
    In effect
    Vote 11–0
  6. Ordinance 2022-005 Enacted 28 Mar 2022

    Independent Food Vendor Insurance Pool

    Sponsored by Councilmember Marcus Okafor, At-Large

    Establishes a city-administered umbrella liability pool that small chefs, cooperatives, and home-kitchen operators may opt into when serving at sanctioned program events. Premiums are scaled to annual revenue and capped for first-year participants.

    Full text (PDF) Vote record
    In effect

    Pandemic Outdoor Dining Extension (Sunset 2024)

    Sponsored by Councilmember Priya Thanawala, District 1

    Granted temporary authority for restaurants and pop-ups to occupy parking lanes and plazas during the public-health emergency, with simplified permitting through the program. Sunset clause triggered on 31 December 2024 and the ordinance was not renewed.

How a meal becomes policy

Process · 5 stages

Every ordinance above traces back to a shared neighborhood meal. The path from a dinner-table conversation to enacted municipal code typically takes 11–18 months and passes through five formal stages.

1
Proposal

A program participant, chef, or neighborhood collective files a written proposal with the Office of Food & Culture.

2
Community input

The proposal is hosted at three open community meals where residents annotate, debate, and amend the draft.

3
Committee review

The relevant Council standing committee reviews the proposal, takes public testimony, and issues a recommendation.

4
Council vote

The full City Council reads, debates, and votes. A simple majority is required for ordinances; resolutions need a quorum.

5
Implementation

The Office drafts administrative rules, publishes them for a 30-day comment period, and enforces them through staff.

Want to start at stage one? Anyone who has attended at least one program meal in the past 12 months may file a proposal.
