Braised Saffron Fennel
with Preserved Lemon & Sumac Crumbs

A study in three slow tempers — the fennel sweats first in olive oil until its anise softens into something almost confectionery, then meets a saffron broth that stains the cut faces an alarming, beautiful gold. We finish with sumac-toasted breadcrumbs for textural argument. Serve warm, not hot; the perfume is better that way.

To finish

  • — gsmall handfulReserved fennel fronds

Method
  1. Heat the oven to 325°F (165°C) with a rack in the middle position. Bloom the saffron in 2 tablespoons of warm (not hot) water and set aside — you want this liquid the colour of a marigold by the time you use it.
    Editor: Yusra — confirm 165°C in both metric and imperial? Style sheet uses parentheses. — RP
  2. Trim the fennel, keeping the root end intact so the halves hold together. Halve each bulb lengthwise. Reserve the feathery fronds in a small bowl of cold water.
  3. Warm 80 ml olive oil in a wide Dutch oven over medium heat. Lay the fennel halves cut-side down in a single layer (work in two batches if needed) and cook, undisturbed, for 6–8 minutes, until deeply golden. Flip and brown the second cut side, 3 minutes more.
    Test note: Batch #4 over-crowded the pan — fennel steamed instead of browning. Spec a 12" / 30 cm pan minimum.
  4. Tuck the smashed garlic, preserved lemon peel, and crushed fennel seed into the gaps between the bulbs. Pour in the wine and let it bubble down by half, scraping any sticky bits from the base.
  5. Add the stock and the bloomed saffron with its soaking liquid. Season lightly — the preserved lemon will season again later. The braise should come about halfway up the fennel.
  6. Cover with parchment pressed to the surface, then the lid. Transfer to the oven and braise for 45–55 minutes, until a paring knife slides into the thickest part with no resistance.
    Copyedit: "Cartouche" preferred over "parchment pressed to the surface" per house style (ch. 1, p. 14). — KM
  7. While the fennel braises, make the crumbs. Warm 2 tbsp olive oil in a small skillet over medium-low heat. Add breadcrumbs and toast, stirring often, until crisp and the colour of buckwheat honey, 5–7 minutes. Off the heat, fold in sumac, Aleppo (if using), lemon zest, and a pinch of salt. Cool on a plate.
  8. To serve, lift the fennel onto a wide, shallow platter. Spoon over a few tablespoons of the braising liquid — not all of it; reserve the rest for soup. Scatter generously with sumac crumbs, the dried fronds, and a final thread of finishing olive oil. Eat warm, not hot.
    Headnote echo: Last line repeats headnote's "warm, not hot." Intentional? Consider cutting one. — RP
